## Findings

- **X1 — BLOCKING: a mid-publication local edit silently drops merged
  remote content from the next publication.** `resumePending` applies the
  published domain with `applyMergedIfGeneration(domain,
  capturedGeneration)`; when a local edit advanced the generation during
  upload/verify/head-advance (or between a crash and its resume), the
  compare-and-set skips and the engine ignores the returned `false`. The
  base shadow still advances to the published snapshot, so the next
  `planCandidate` pass skips the frontier remote equal to the base shadow
  and builds the next snapshot from a journal that never absorbed the
  remote branch. Reproduced at review with the phase's own harness
  components: device B publishes entry R; device A merges R into its
  candidate and edits locally after head advance; afterwards A's journal
  lacks R, A's next published head snapshot lacks R with **no tombstone**,
  A reports `up-to-date`, and a fresh device C joining the vault never
  receives R. Only device B syncing again heals every replica; if B is
  retired after its last publish — the core backup-restore scenario — R is
  permanently unreachable through heads. This defeats the plan-v7 §8
  no-lost-update obligation despite all 30 gate scenarios passing: the
  existing generation-CAS scenario (`device-cas`) uses only local content,
  which the skip cannot lose. Obligation: the engine must guarantee that
  everything contained in its own settled base shadow is either applied to
  the journal or re-merged into the next candidate — no publication may
  regress content relative to its own parent without a tombstone. Fix
  design is the implementer's, but the review probe is the acceptance
  test: remote-derived entry + mid-publication edit ⇒ the next published
  snapshot, the local journal, and a fresh restoring device must all
  contain the remote entry.
- **X2 (minor, recorded):** a corrupted pending-publication blob makes
  `decodeSnapshotV2` throw out of `resumePending` unclassified —
  `handleExpectedFailure` rethrows it instead of mapping it to a durable
  Attention reason, so local candidate corruption crashes the sync pass
  rather than pausing it. Tighten when next touching the engine.
- **X3 (nit, V7-5 note):** the iOS `AtomicFileModule` uses
  `FileHandle.synchronize`/`fsync`; Apple documents `F_FULLFSYNC` as the
  primitive for true power-loss durability. Covered today by the gate's
  physical-device non-claim; revisit during V7-5 device hardening.
