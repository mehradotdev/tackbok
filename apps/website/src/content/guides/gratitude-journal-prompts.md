A blank page can make gratitude feel like homework. A good question makes it easier to start. Here are 90 prompts, grouped by time of day and by topic, so you can pick one that fits how today went.

Six of the groups are the prompts built into the Tackbok app, word for word, and each of those groups says so. We wrote the other four groups for this page.

## How to use these prompts

- **Pick one prompt.** One question answered well beats five answered in a hurry.
- **Be specific.** "The barista remembered my order" is easier to picture later than "nice people".
- **Keep it short.** A few sentences is enough. Some days one line is enough.
- **Repeat freely.** The same prompt gets a different answer on a different day.
- **Skip what doesn't fit.** If a prompt feels forced today, choose another.

## Small moments

Built into Tackbok as the **Little things** focus area: the small, often overlooked good things in an ordinary day.

1. What made you smile today?
2. What small moment made you pause today?
3. What everyday comfort would you miss the most?
4. Describe a small, everyday detail around you that you find beautiful.
5. What is your favorite sound to hear when you wake up in the morning?
6. Write about a simple pleasure that you look forward to every day.
7. What was the best part of your morning routine today?
8. Describe a brief encounter with a stranger that brought you joy.
9. What is an inexpensive item that brings a lot of value to your life?

## Morning

New prompts for the start of the day, when you're setting your attention for what comes next.

1. What is one thing you're looking forward to today, however small?
2. What is something about this morning that you would usually rush past?
3. Who are you likely to see today that you're glad to have around?
4. What strength or skill will help you through today?
5. What do you have now that you once hoped for?
6. Which part of your home makes mornings a little easier?
7. What would make today feel worthwhile, even if nothing else goes to plan?
8. What did you do yesterday that made today easier?
9. What is one kind thing you could do for someone today?

## Evening

New prompts for the end of the day. They work well as a short wind-down before bed.

1. What was the best moment of today, and who was part of it?
2. What went better today than you expected?
3. Who helped you today, even in a small way?
4. What did today teach you that you didn't expect?
5. What did you get done today that you can feel good about?
6. Which ordinary part of today would you like to remember a year from now?
7. What conversation from today are you glad you had?
8. What comfort is waiting for you tonight before you sleep?
9. What are you glad you said yes, or no, to today?

## Yourself

Built into Tackbok as the **Self** focus area: your growth, your strengths and what you need.

1. What do you need more of in your life?
2. What is something that was hard to do but you did it anyway?
3. What would you tell your younger self today?
4. What is a recent accomplishment that you haven't celebrated enough?
5. Write down three things you love about your personality.
6. How have you grown from a mistake you made recently?
7. What boundary do you need to set to protect your energy?
8. In what area of your life do you feel the most authentic?
9. Describe your ideal day from morning to night.

## Health and body

Built into Tackbok as the **Health** focus area: your body, rest and the habits that look after you.

1. What part of your body are you most thankful for today?
2. How has rest helped you lately?
3. What healthy habit are you proud of keeping?
4. What is a nourishing meal that always makes you feel good?
5. Describe a physical activity that brings you joy rather than feeling like a chore.
6. How does your body tell you when it needs to slow down or rest?
7. What are you doing today to care for your mental well-being?
8. Write about a time you overcame a physical challenge or injury.
9. What small change can you make to improve your sleep quality?

## Family

Built into Tackbok as the **Family** focus area: the people you grew up with or chose as family, and the moments you share.

1. What family tradition are you grateful for?
2. Who in your family taught you something lasting?
3. What is a small moment with family you want to remember?
4. What is a story from your family's history that you find inspiring?
5. How has your relationship with a family member evolved over time?
6. Write about a skill or a recipe that has been passed down in your family.
7. What is a specific personality trait you share with a parent or sibling?
8. Describe a childhood memory that still brings you immense joy.
9. How does your family support each other during difficult times?

## Friends

Built into Tackbok as the **Friends** focus area: the friends who support you, and what you've learned from them.

1. Which friend has made your life lighter recently?
2. What is a memory with a friend that still makes you smile?
3. Who do you want to encourage this week?
4. What quality do you value most in your closest friendships?
5. Write about a friend who helped you see things from a different perspective.
6. How do you prefer to show your appreciation and love to your friends?
7. Who is a friend you haven't spoken to in a while, and what would you say to them?
8. Describe a fun or unexpected adventure you had with a friend.
9. What is a lesson you've learned from one of your friendships?

## Work and study

New prompts for the part of life you spend working, learning or looking after a household.

1. Which colleague or classmate made your day easier recently?
2. What part of your work are you quietly good at?
3. What problem did you solve this week that you're proud of?
4. Who taught you something you still use at work or in your studies?
5. What tool, space or routine makes your work smoother?
6. What feedback helped you improve, even if it was hard to hear?
7. What are you learning right now that you'll be glad to know later?
8. What does your work make possible for you or the people you care about?
9. When did you last handle a busy day in a way you're proud of?

## Hard days

New prompts for days when gratitude feels out of reach. Being grateful for something doesn't mean the hard part isn't real, and you don't have to feel better after writing. If none of these fit today, it's fine to close the journal.

If you're struggling and need support, please reach out to someone you trust or to a local helpline.

1. What is one thing that went okay today, even if the rest didn't?
2. Who could you lean on right now, even a little?
3. What has helped you get through a hard time before?
4. What small comfort is within reach right now?
5. What did you manage today, even if it was only getting through it?
6. What is a kind thing you can say to yourself tonight?
7. What helps you feel even a little calmer?
8. What is something steady in your life that you can count on?
9. What are you looking forward to once this hard stretch has passed?

## Faith

Built into Tackbok as the **Faith** focus area, for people who find meaning in faith or spirituality. It's optional in the app, like every focus area.

1. What's your earliest memory of feeling God's presence?
2. Where have you seen grace in your life recently?
3. Which prayer helped you through a difficult time?
4. How has your faith changed the way you view challenges?
5. What spiritual practice or habit brings you the most peace?
6. Write about a time when you felt clearly guided by a higher power.
7. What is a specific teaching or quote that inspires your daily life?
8. How do you find spiritual connection in the midst of a busy week?
9. Reflect on a moment when your faith offered you comfort during uncertainty.

## Using these prompts in Tackbok

<!-- Screenshot slot (#194): the new entry screen with a prompt as its title, and Settings › Journaling › Journal focus areas. -->

- **Choose your topics.** Under Settings › Journaling › Journal focus areas, pick at least two of Self, Little things, Health, Family, Friends and Faith. Tackbok starts with Self and Little things.
- **Start with a prompt.** While writing an entry, tap **Add prompt** to use one from your topics as the title, or **New prompt** to shuffle. To have every new entry start with a prompt, choose **Built-in prompts** under Settings › Journaling › Journal prompts.
- **Add your own.** Save any prompt you like, such as those in the Morning, Evening, Work and study or Hard days groups above. With a prompt in an entry's title, tap **Show all**, then **My prompts › Create prompt**. Under Journal prompts, choose **My prompts** to use only yours, or **All prompts** to mix them with the built-in ones.
- **Get a daily reminder.** Turn on Settings › Notifications › Daily reminder, and each reminder shows a prompt from your topics. Tap it to start an entry with that prompt.
